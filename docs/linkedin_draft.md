I've been in Biomaterials this semester, and after a site visit at Oceanit I kept thinking about their butterfly-wing-inspired armor work — lightweight, flexible, high strength-to-weight. I'm not affiliated with Oceanit; I just left that tour wanting to understand the idea at a scale I can actually print and analyze.

So I started a concept study: a Morpho-inspired panel with a lower skin, an upper skin, and a ridge / cross-rib core (the architecture in published Morpho and papilionid scale papers, scaled up a few thousand times). I wrote parametric CAD for three lattice densities plus a solid control plate of the same footprint, all meant to print flat on my Bambu X1C Cool Plate in PLA with no supports.

I also ran a computational stiffness-to-weight comparison (beam theory plus a small linear-elastic FEA) and wrote a 3-point-bend / low-energy drop protocol I'll run after I print. To be clear, a PLA coupon is not armor, and I don't have physical test data yet — the repo labels every stiffness number as a computational estimate.

Code, STLs, figures, and the write-up are here: https://github.com/michaellozadalongog/biomimetic-butterfly-lattice

If you've worked on bio-inspired lattices or cellular solids, I'd love feedback.
